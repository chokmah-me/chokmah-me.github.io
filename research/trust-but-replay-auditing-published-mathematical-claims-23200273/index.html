Abstract

Published mathematics is trusted far more than it is independently replayed. Peer review checks reasoning, not computation, and increasingly part of the proof is a computation. This paper describes a six-day audit campaign (18 to 23 September 2026) that replays published mathematical claims from scratch under a hostile prior: every locked gate is built to produce the opposite verdict where the opposite is correct, and a CI-enforced verdict lock fails loudly on drift in either direction.

The contribution is the playbook: a five-disposition taxonomy (BREAK, GAP, PASS, SKIP, UNKNOWN), seven attack types classified by mechanism, and the evidentiary disciplines (paper-first gating, discrimination controls, gate-before-prove, fail-closed replay, independent anchors), together with the record of where those disciplines were violated. The 32 locked gates (17 BREAK, 15 PASS) fall into historical calibrations, live-literature targets, and low-stakes preprints. Gates refute routes, not theorems.
