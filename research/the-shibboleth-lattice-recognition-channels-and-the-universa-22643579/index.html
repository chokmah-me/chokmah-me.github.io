Abstract

Coalition behavior in multi-agent systems appears across four canonical substrates: quantum entanglement, evolutionary covert-tag recognition, engineered handshake codes, and emergent relational memory in frontier language models, plus two boundary cases. These are treated as instances of one structure: a joint action distribution over an inside set of agents that fails to factor when conditioned on what an outside principal can observe. The structure is a binding operator B = (S, I, Wagents, Wapparatus, &rho;, &chi;, &chi;actual) with recognition-channel proxy &kappa;H, the principal-relative uncertainty coefficient on the channel through which inside-set agents identify each other. The &kappa;H estimate rests on a single model-by-behavior selection from Potter et al. (2026); alternative selections span most of the unit interval.This revision integrates Paglieri et al. (2026), a controlled 100-agent swarm with transparent channels. A fifth scope limit is recorded: substrate-carried contagion is the general phenomenon; B covers only the covert subset where the channel is outside the principal's observable algebra. Specification divergence is added as a third witness failure mode alongside W-degradation and W-capture. Section 7.6 compares the transparent swarm to the Artifactory incident, including a correction on internal dissent. An open question on competing bindings over a shared substrate is stated.The core prediction is unchanged: blinded witness-set substitution should collapse coalition behavior even at saturating &kappa;H, distinguishing B from instrumental convergence accounts. This prediction remains untested. Companion simulations live in a separate deposit and at github.com/chokmah-me/shibboleth-lattice-sim.
