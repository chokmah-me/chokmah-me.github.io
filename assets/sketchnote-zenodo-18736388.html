SHADOW PRICE ON MIR

Compiler-as-NUM hypothesis, first MIR inlining test. v1.1

23 February 2026 · 3,000 inlining decisions

A JIT doing profile-guided inlining is dual decomposition: the runtime posts a price, and the optimizer spends where the price is high.

KEY INSIGHT

Shadow price and the inlining decision move together. Sensitivity depends on the optimization level. This is not the later five-condition speedup study (10.5281/zenodo.18828679).

ACROSS 3,000 DECISIONS

Spearman ρ = 0.71
Cohen d = 2.00
98.4% agreement at ±20% price

750 decisions in each of four conditions. p < 10⁻¹¹⁴.

WHERE IT WEAKENS

  • O0: ρ = 0.50, threshold ceiling.
  • O1 and O2: ρ = 0.82.
  • Decision boundary at 100 to 200 IR instructions.

V1.1 SCOPE

The standalone decision function is a passed falsification test, not a full identification of the compiler. Predictions named here: O(1/√k) for cross-module price propagation, and O(log n) stability for adaptive inlining.

LIMIT

Underdetermination remains. The five-condition Cohen d of 2.65 to 4.42 is the later MIR record, not this one.
